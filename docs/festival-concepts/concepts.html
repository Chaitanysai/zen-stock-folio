ZenFolio · Festival experience v2 · Concepts for review · 8 Nov 2026

Four festival concepts, live.

These are working motion prototypes running the same particle engine I plan to ship. They are mockups of the ZenFolio hero and dashboard, not the real pages. Nothing in src/ has been changed yet. Use the toggles under each frame to switch theme, replay the opening sequence, and show the keep-clear zones.

What I found in the code

Event engine: keep as is. lib/events (provider, registry, resolver, IST dates, ±1-day window, curated fallback) is clean and React-free. useActiveEvent, usePrefersReducedMotion and useDocumentHidden are reusable. The registry is typed against AnimationId and lazy-loads each animation.
Why signed-in users saw nothing. App.tsx renders / as <Landing/> when signed out and <Index/> (the dashboard) when signed in. EventAnimationLayer is mounted only inside Landing's hero, so the dashboard never got it.
Shared integration point. I'll add one EventExperienceProvider in App.tsx above the router. It resolves the event once and exposes {active, reducedMotion, paused, dismissed, setDismissed}. Landing, the dashboard and Login only consume it, so the date logic exists in one place.
Dashboard structure. .zf → Rail + .zf-stage → sticky TopBar → main → .zf-page-head (greeting h1) → cards. The overview has a HomeAtmosphere night-to-day intro that plays once per session. The festival ribbon goes into .zf-page-head on the overview tab only and waits until that intro has finished.

Not touched: auth, Firestore, the backend, and the calendar data. Motion for React is not installed. I recommend not adding it: the effects are particle work on one <canvas> plus a few CSS transitions, and an extra library (about 35 kB gzipped) would not change how they look.

Why v1 failed

Changes in v2
• Motion is the main event: rockets, bursts and drifting petals, with a choreographed opening and then a calm loop.
• Composition follows the layout. Effects use the open sky above the HUD, the top-right corner and the far edges. The copy column and the HUD/chart get keep-clear zones, and particles fade out as they enter them.
• Each audience gets its own version. Visitors see a full-hero celebration. Signed-in users get a restrained greeting ribbon on the dashboard.
• The user can dismiss the animation (✕) or pause it; the greeting stays.
• One canvas per surface instead of dozens of SVG nodes. It caps devicePixelRatio, stops when the tab is hidden, and draws one static frame under reduced motion.

Reference board

I studied each site live in a headless browser: computed type, animation counts and timings, and a screenshot. Blocked: landing.love, lapa.ninja and land-book.com (Cloudflare/Vercel bot walls), plus Mobbin and Screenlane app screens (sign-in required). For those I used only the public pages and say so below. OpenPencil: I installed @open-pencil/cli@0.16.0 and ran a smoke test that imported an HTML greeting chip to .fig and exported it to PNG, which worked. The motion concepts themselves are built in HTML and canvas, because OpenPencil is a static vector editor and cannot preview particles. I did not use it for the artwork.

CompositionThree depth layers. Back: a plum-to-saffron dusk wash from the top-right and distant fireworks at 45% scale. Middle: rockets rise through the open column between the copy and the HUD, and the full-size bursts open in the sky above the HUD. Front: a marigold toran just under the nav (scallops with hanging drops and mango leaves), drifting petals, and five small diyas along the bottom edge, three under the copy and two in the open column. The headline, CTA, HUD and chart are keep-clear zones, and any particle that enters one fades to 10%.
Choreography0–0.9 s: the dusk wash fades in. 0.3–1.2 s: the toran drops in with a little spring. 0.6 s: the first petals appear. 1.4 s: the first rocket, bursting at about 2.3 s. 2.6 s: a second rocket. 3.0 s: a distant burst. 4.2 s: a paired launch. From about 6 s it loops: a rocket every 2.4–4.6 s, at most 3 bursts on screen, flames flicker, petals keep falling.
PaletteGold #FFC85A, saffron #FF8A32, magenta #FF46A0, cyan #50DCFF, warm white #FFF4DC. The light theme uses deeper inks of the same hues (#D6960A, #EC6814, #D62476, #0096BE), painted normally rather than additively, so the bursts stay vivid on the pale background.
ParticlesFive burst shapes: peony (a sphere), tilted ring, gold willow (slow, long-lived, droops), palm (7 arms) and chrysanthemum (white core turning to colour). Each spark is a streak whose length follows its speed. Exponential drag plus gravity gives the natural spread and droop. Budget: 900 sparks on desktop, 22 petals, 5 diyas, one canvas.
Prominent vs ambientProminent: the rockets, the bursts and the opening sequence. Ambient: the toran sway (period 6 s), petal drift, diya flames and the dusk wash.
InteractionA greeting chip at the top right of the hero has ❚❚ (pause) and ✕ (hide). Hiding fades the canvas out over 400 ms and stops the loop, and the chip stays as "Shubh Deepavali". The choice lasts for the session only (sessionStorage), with no backend. Optional extra: clicking empty sky launches a rocket to that spot, at most one every 0.6 s. Clicks on text or buttons are unaffected.

CompositionThe ribbon is not a banner. The celebration lives in empty space that already exists in the overview header: the gap between "Good evening, Chait" and the price clock. A thin marigold toran hangs from the very top edge behind the top-bar pills. A "Happy Diwali" chip with a flickering diya sits next to the price clock. Small distant fireworks and a few petals appear only in the header gap. It adds 0 px of height, so nothing moves.
ChoreographyIt waits until the existing HomeAtmosphere night-to-day intro has finished. Then: the toran fades in over 0–0.6 s, the chip appears at 0.4 s, one distant burst follows at 1.2 s, and from there one burst every 3.6–6.5 s with at most 2 on screen. After 20 s the burst rate halves.
PaletteThe same hues, at roughly 60% of the public alpha. No dusk wash, so the dashboard's background and P&L day-glow stay exactly as they are.
ParticlesFireworks at the 45% and 70% depth tiers only (no front-tier bursts), budget 260 sparks, about 7 petals. Every density scales with the actual size of the gap, so a narrow window gets less.
Prominent vs ambientProminent: the chip, the one thing that says "Diwali". Ambient: everything else. No effect ever crosses a card, a number or a table.
Other signed-in pagesThe minimal variant: the static toran and the chip only. No canvas runs on Portfolio, Trades, Journal, Settings or other forms, tables and dialogs. Login and signup also get the minimal chip.

Implementation plan (after approval)

Keepsrc/lib/events/* and its tests are unchanged. useActiveEvent, usePrefersReducedMotion and useDocumentHidden are reused.
Addevents/EventExperienceProvider.tsx mounted in App.tsx around the routes, holding the resolution, reduced motion, hidden state and session dismissal. Also EventGreeting.tsx (chip with pause and hide), EventAnimationLayer taking a variant: "public-hero" | "authenticated-dashboard" | "minimal" prop, and engine/ (canvas loop, spark pool, zone fade, DPR cap, visibility and IntersectionObserver pause). Diwali parts: Rocket, FireworkBurst, FlowerPetals, Toran, Diya. Christmas parts: SnowField, ShootingStars, LightString, Aurora, Pines. Each event animation stays its own lazy chunk.
ReplaceThe v1 visual parts (Rangoli, LightParticles, StarField, AmbientGlow, EvergreenRidge, old Diya/SnowField) and their rules in events.css. Component tests are updated to match.
Mount pointsLanding hero uses public-hero. Dashboard overview .zf-page-head uses authenticated-dashboard and waits for HomeAtmosphere to reach day. Other dashboard tabs and Login/Signup use minimal. The resolver never sees the variant.
PerformanceOne canvas per surface, DPR ≤ 1.75, spark pool with a hard budget, no animated CSS filters, only transform and opacity in the DOM, loop stopped when hidden or off-screen, and one static frame under reduced motion.
VerifyPlaywright against vite preview with dates pinned through a test hook: Diwali −1, 0 and +1, the Christmas window, and no event. Covers public, dashboard (signed-in session stubbed at the auth context; no real credentials), login and signup, light and dark, 1440/834/390, reduced motion, dismissal, overflow, console errors and keep-clear overlap checks. Screenshots plus short WebM recordings for all four combinations. Then npm test.
